# MoMo (M_Service JSC): business model, ecosystem, products, financials, strategy and regulation (as of Nov 2026)

Legend used in these notes: **[V]** = official, from MoMo, the regulator or a legal text. **[R]** = press reporting on filings or documents (e.g., DealStreetAsia, Vietdata, business registration). **[E]** = third-party estimate or survey. **[I]** = my own inference. Dates are publication dates unless stated otherwise. MoMo is unlisted and does not publish audited financials, so every financial figure below comes from third parties.

## 1. Business model: how MoMo makes money

### Takeaway
MoMo is a licensed payment intermediary (e-wallet, collection/disbursement support, payment gateway). It cannot lend or pay interest itself. Most consumer-facing services are free, so it earns from the supply side: merchant and biller fees, commissions for distributing partner banks' and finance companies' credit (Ví Trả Sau, Vay Nhanh), insurance, savings and investment products, and marketing/ads services. **No reliable public breakdown of revenue by business line exists.**

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Licences:** MoMo is operated by Công ty Cổ phần Dịch vụ Di động Trực tuyến (M_Service). E-wallet licence No. 16/GP-NHNN issued by SBV on 16/10/2015. Certifications: ISO 27001, ISO 27701, PCI DSS Level 1. Page updated 14/10/2026 — [momo.vn/llms.txt](https://www.momo.vn/llms.txt)
- **[V]** Its licensed payment intermediary services are e-wallet, "hỗ trợ thu hộ, chi hộ" (collection/disbursement support) and e-payment gateway — [momo.careers/about](https://momo.careers/about)
- **[V] Partners provide the credit.** Ví Trả Sau is provided by credit institutions TPBank, MBV and VCBNeo. MoMo acts as an intermediary that supports collection and disbursement — [momo.vn/vi-tra-sau](https://www.momo.vn/vi-tra-sau). MoMo's documentation says Ví Trả Sau, Vay Nhanh, insurance, fund certificates and deposits are "offered by licensed partners on the MoMo app" (search snippet of MoMo FAQ pages; e.g., [momo.vn/hoi-dap Túi Thần Tài](https://www.momo.vn/hoi-dap/tui-than-tai-la-gi))
- **[V/R] Legal limit on the model:** Clause 5, Article 25 of Circular 40/2024/TT-NHNN bars e-wallet providers from extending credit to wallet users or paying interest on balances. Balances sit in a payment-guarantee account at a partner bank. To earn lending or interest-spread income, wallets must work through partner credit institutions and share revenue — [VietnamFinance, 24/08/2026](https://vietnamfinance.vn/thu-phi-nguoi-dung-bai-toan-kho-cua-vi-dien-tu-d149537.html). Tuổi Trẻ, reporting on Circular 41/2025, also says top-ups and withdrawals must go through a linked bank account, providers may not take cash deposits or allow cash withdrawals at their locations, and may not extend credit or pay interest — [Tuổi Trẻ, 10/11/2025](https://tuoitre.vn/plo/tu-1-1-2026-muon-mo-vi-dien-tu-phai-xac-minh-thong-tin-sinh-trac-hoc-post880385.html)
- **[R] Fee structure as of Aug 2026:**
  - Free: P2P transfers, mobile top-up, bill payment, opening and using the wallet.
  - Charged: top-ups by international card, bank withdrawals beyond the free count or limit, and cash withdrawals at top-up points under a separate schedule.
  - Source: [VietnamFinance, 24/08/2026](https://vietnamfinance.vn/thu-phi-nguoi-dung-bai-toan-kho-cua-vi-dien-tu-d149537.html). Note: cash withdrawal at points seems to conflict with the Circular 41 no-cash rule reported by Tuổi Trẻ above. This is unresolved.
- **[R] Where profit comes from:** VietnamFinance calls MoMo "the only e-wallet currently profitable". It says the profit comes from the super-app model (consumer lending, savings, investment, bill payment, lifestyle) through cross-selling and engagement, not from transaction fees alone. By comparison, ShopeePay earns inside the Shopee flow and VNPay earns from QR and payment infrastructure for banks and merchants — [VietnamFinance, 24/08/2026](https://vietnamfinance.vn/thu-phi-nguoi-dung-bai-toan-kho-cua-vi-dien-tu-d149537.html)
- **[E] Revenue options for wallets (economist Trần Nguyên Đán):**
  - Shift revenue to the supply side: merchant fees, financial-product distribution fees (e.g., micro-insurance), in-app advertising.
  - Paid value-added services: recurring auto-payments for household businesses and SMEs, cash-flow reconciliation, international transfers, seller tools.
  - Direct user fees, which suit only narrow segments. He thinks mass account-maintenance fees are unlikely.
  - Source: [VietnamFinance, 24/08/2026](https://vietnamfinance.vn/thu-phi-nguoi-dung-bai-toan-kho-cua-vi-dien-tu-d149537.html)
- **[R] 2024 cost structure (DealStreetAsia, from regulatory filings):** revenue above VND 3,300bn (+27% YoY); COGS VND 386.1bn (−8%); selling expenses VND 1,990bn; G&A VND 834bn; financial expenses VND 8.1bn (−34.2%); net profit VND 347.5bn — [BSA/DealStreetAsia, 14/05/2025](https://www.bsa.org.vn/momo-co-lai-trong-ca-nam-2024-tai-khoi-dong-ke-hoach-ipo/)

- **[R] Securities:** On 9/6/2022 M_Service bought over 4.4M shares, or 49%, of Chứng khoán CV (CVS). CVS was loss-making by about VND 140bn as of 30/9/2025 — [VnBusiness, 13/01/2026](https://vnbusiness.vn/bat-ngo-voi-so-von-dieu-le-cua-ky-lan-cong-nghe-momo.html)

### Inferences
- [I] On the DealStreetAsia 2024 figures, gross margin is about 88% ((3,300−386)/3,300). Selling expenses of VND 1,990bn are about 60% of revenue. This looks like a high-gross-margin commission/fee business, with promotions and marketing as its largest cost. AI that improves the ROI of promotion spend (targeting, personalisation, cutting cashback leakage) would hit the largest P&L line directly.
- [I] Net margin in 2024 was about 10.5% (347.5/3,300), so profitability is real but thin.
- [I] Because MoMo cannot lend, the credit value chain is: MoMo data, distribution and scoring support → partner bank or finance company decides and funds → MoMo earns a fee or revenue share. An AI credit product would need to fit this sponsoring-bank model. VCBNeo explicitly calls itself a "sponsoring bank" for fintechs.
- [I] The lending scale (above USD 700M disbursed in 2024) against investment AUM (above USD 300M) suggests credit distribution is a major monetisation line. This is not confirmed by any disclosed revenue split.

### Gaps
- No source discloses revenue share by line (payments/MDR, bills, top-up, credit distribution, insurance, investment, ads). I found none published by MoMo or credible press. A Studocu student paper claims a 2024 breakdown, but it is not credible and its chart was not visible.
- MDR or merchant fee levels for MoMo QR or gateway were not found.
- The take rate on Ví Trả Sau and Vay Nhanh, and the revenue-share terms with TPBank, MBV, VCBNeo and other partners, are not public.

## 2. Scale: users, merchants, partners, volumes and market share

### Takeaway
MoMo consistently says it has **over 30 million users** (2024–2026). A securities report put it at **over 40M registered accounts and about 10M MAU in 2024**. It has **over 500,000 merchants and household businesses** on MoMo QR (late 2025–2026) and links with **about 70 banks and financial institutions**. MoMo remains the most-used standalone fintech (Decision Lab penetration 61% in Q4 2024 and 64% in Q2 2025). However, market-share numbers vary widely by source and method, and bank apps are gaining.

### Inferences
- [I] "30M+ users" against 10M MAU (TVS 2024) implies that only about a quarter to a third of the base is monthly-active. Activation and engagement of dormant users is an obvious AI target (personalised next-best-action).
- [I] MoMo's own "30M+ users" claim barely moved from 2024 to Nov 2026, while SBV active wallets nationally held flat at about 30.3M from Mar to Sep 2025. Both point to a saturating user base, so growth must come from monetisation per user (credit, wealth, insurance, merchants) rather than new users.
- [I] The merchant base grew from 300,000 acceptance points (Oct 2024 and Jul 2025) to over 500,000 merchants on QR (Nov–Dec 2025). The two numbers may use different definitions, so this is not necessarily organic growth.

## 3. Financials (2022–2025), fundraising, valuation and IPO plans

### Takeaway
After years of losses (about VND 1,150bn in 2022 and about VND 287bn in 2023, per Vietdata), MoMo posted its **first full-year profit in 2024: VND 347.5bn net on about VND 3,300bn revenue**, per DealStreetAsia citing regulatory filings. It reportedly **stayed profitable in 2025**, but no figure has been published. The last primary round was the **USD 200M Series E in Dec 2021 led by Mizuho, at a valuation above USD 2bn**. In 2026, shareholders explored a **secondary sale of up to 50% at up to USD 3bn**, advised by Morgan Stanley and Jefferies. An IPO is "not on the near-term agenda".

## 6. Regulatory environment affecting AI product design

### Takeaway
The rules allow AI for fraud detection, eKYC and personalisation, but constrain it.
- **E-wallet rules** (Decree 52/2024, Circular 40/2024 amended by Circular 41/2025, Decision 2345/2024): caps of VND 100M/month, or 300M for essential services; mandatory biometrics; no wallet credit or interest; bank-linked top-ups.
- **Personal Data Protection Law 91/2025 + Decree 356/2025** (effective 1/1/2026): financial transaction history and biometrics are sensitive data; **using credit information for credit scoring requires the data subject's consent**.
- **AI Law 134/2025** (effective 1/3/2026): risk-tiered obligations, mandatory disclosure for chatbots and labelling of AI content, high-risk conformity assessment and strict compensation, and an 18-month transition for finance (to about Sept 2027).
- **Decree 94/2025 sandbox** (from 1/7/2025): credit scoring, Open API data sharing and P2P lending can be tested.
- **Circular 64/2024 Open API** (from 1/3/2025): standardises bank APIs, including wallet top-up and withdrawal.

### Inferences
- [I] **Credit scoring:** an AI credit model using MoMo transaction data needs explicit, specific consent under PDPL Art. 27 and Decree 356. Financial transaction history is sensitive data. The credit decision legally sits with the partner bank, since MoMo cannot lend. A model for a new kind of credit-scoring service could go through the Decree 94 sandbox. A credit-scoring or automated lending model is a plausible candidate for the AI Law's "high-risk" tier, which would require conformity assessment, logs, human oversight and strict compensation liability. This is unconfirmed because the high-risk list is unverified.
- [I] **Conversational AI (Moni, agents):** it must disclose that it is AI (AI Law). Any action that moves money stays subject to the biometric thresholds (above VND 10M per transaction or 20M per day) and the wallet caps (100M/300M per month). An "agentic" transfer feature therefore needs a human-in-the-loop biometric step for large amounts.
- [I] **Biometrics and eKYC anti-deepfake models** (Flashmask/FlashMark) process sensitive biometric data, which requires a sensitive-data impact assessment and possibly a DPO under Decree 356. Retention is mandated by Circular 41.
- [I] **Open API** (Circular 64) lets MoMo, as a payment intermediary, pull bank data and initiate payments with consent. This enables cross-bank personal finance management (PFM) or cash-flow AI. Actual bank readiness is unknown.
- [I] **Generative content** for merchants (e.g., AI-made QR art, marketing images) must carry AI labels or tags once tagging rules are issued.

## 7. Known strategic challenges

### Takeaway
The main documented pressures are:
- free, instant interbank transfers and VietQR, which commoditise P2P and QR payments;
- bank super-apps gaining share as the "primary" platform;
- a user base that has largely plateaued;
- thin profitability, a heavy promotions bill, and an inability to charge users mass fees;
- regulatory constraints (no direct lending, biometric friction, multiple regulators, IPO rules on accumulated losses);
- rising online fraud (VND 8,000bn lost in 2025).
